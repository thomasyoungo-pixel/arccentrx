01·AI Strategy

Your AI should be producing operating value. Is it?

Most companies can tell you what they spend on AI. Far fewer can tell you what it returned. The spend is real, the pilots are live, and somewhere between the license and the workflow the value quietly went missing. Before you approve the next round of investment, these are the questions worth answering honestly.

Questions worth answering
  • What are we actually spending across licenses, infrastructure, and the people maintaining it?
  • Who is using it, how often, and for what? What did we buy twice?
  • Which workflows changed, and which ones look the same as they did before?
  • If we had to defend the ROI to the board next quarter, could we?
What we deliver

We answer those questions with numbers, not opinions. Then we fix what's broken, retire what isn't earning its keep, and build the operating model around what works.

What's inside
  • AI spend and utilization audit
  • Use case prioritization
  • Workflow redesign
  • AI remediation and consolidation
  • Model governance and risk controls
  • Build versus buy evaluation
  • Team enablement
02·Growth Strategy

Growth is a system, not a target.

When the number won't move, the instinct is to add pipeline. Often the constraint is upstream of pipeline: pricing that doesn't match value, a segment that was never a fit, a handoff that loses deals between marketing and sales, or a product that converts everywhere except where you're spending.

Questions worth answering
  • Which segment actually converts, retains, and expands? Are we spending there?
  • Where in the funnel does the loss happen, and do we have the data to know?
  • Is pricing a reflection of value or a reflection of history?
  • What would have to be true for the next market to work?
What we deliver

A growth plan tied to the operating reality, with the sales infrastructure to run it.

What's inside
  • Segmentation and ICP
  • Pricing and packaging
  • Sales infrastructure and CRM
  • Funnel and conversion analysis
  • Demand generation
  • Market entry and expansion
  • Partnership and channel strategy
  • Brand and positioning
03·Technology Infrastructure

The architecture you choose now decides what you can do in three years.

Payments, ledgers, KYC, sponsor bank relationships, and data architecture are not vendor decisions. They are ceiling decisions. The wrong one is survivable at fifty thousand customers, structural at five hundred thousand, and catastrophic above a million. Our team has built, managed, and run technology infrastructure inside American Express, Square, Citibank, Apple, and Walmart.

Questions worth answering
  • Does our architecture survive 10x volume, or do we already know where it breaks?
  • Can we add a product, a market, or a partner without adding a vendor?
  • Where does customer and payment data live, who can reach it, and can we prove that?
  • What in our stack is a decision we made once and never revisited?
What we deliver

We help you make these calls with people who have already lived with the consequences of making them, and we stay through the build.

What's inside
  • Architecture and scalability review
  • Payments and ledger design
  • KYC and onboarding infrastructure
  • Sponsor bank and partner strategy
  • Data architecture and residency
  • System rationalization and consolidation
  • Build versus buy and vendor selection

04·Operational Optimization

Throughput, quality, and cost. Measured before and after.

Cost reduction that damages the business is easy. Improvement that holds is harder, because it requires knowing which steps create value and which ones exist because of something that stopped being true. We measure first, then change what the measurement points to.

  • Where does rework come from, and how often?
  • What have we automated on top of a process we should have redesigned?

05·M&A Readiness and Integration

The deal is decided at signing. The return is decided after.

Most of the value in a transaction is won or lost in the ninety days nobody planned for. Sell side, that means being ready before the diligence request arrives instead of assembling answers under a clock. Buy side, it means knowing what you're inheriting and having an integration plan that survives contact with two real organizations.

Questions worth answering
  • If diligence started next month, what would we be scrambling to produce?
  • What in the target's stack, contracts, or compliance posture becomes our problem on day one?
  • Who owns integration, and is that a real role or a second job?
  • What synergies did we underwrite, and how will we know if they landed?
What we deliver

Readiness work that raises what a buyer sees, and integration work that protects what a buyer paid for.

What's inside
  • Sell side readiness and data room preparation
  • Buy side technology and operations diligence
  • Integration planning and execution
  • Systems and platform consolidation
  • Org and role mapping
  • Synergy tracking
  • Carve out and separation support
06·Compliance and Governance

Regulated doesn't have to mean slow.

In healthcare and financial services, compliance is where good roadmaps go to stall. Usually it isn't the regulation that slows you down. It's discovering the requirement late, treating every control as a one off, and rebuilding evidence by hand every audit cycle. Teams who have done it before design for audit that stands up to repeated validation, and keep moving.

Questions worth answering
  • Is compliance built into how we ship, or is it a scramble before every audit?
  • Which deals or partnerships are we losing to a certification we don't have yet?
  • Who owns governance for the AI and the data we've put into production?
  • If a regulator, acquirer, or partner asked for evidence tomorrow, how long would it take?
What we deliver

We build the controls, the documentation, and the governance so the answer is days, not quarters, and so the next audit costs a fraction of the last one.

What's inside
  • SOC 2
  • HIPAA
  • PCI
  • ADA and accessibility
  • State licensing
  • Model and AI governance
  • Control design and evidence automation
  • Audit readiness and remediation
  • Third party and vendor risk
  • Policy and documentation
07·Organizational Design

The org chart is usually a record of the last stage, not the next one.

Structure quietly decides speed. Roles accumulate, ownership blurs, and the same decision gets made in three places or none. Most companies feel this as a performance problem long before they see it as a structure problem.

Questions worth answering
  • Who owns this decision, and does everyone agree on that answer?
  • Where does work stop moving, and is that a person, a process, or a reporting line?
  • Are we structured for the revenue we have or the revenue we're planning for?
  • What roles are we about to hire that the structure would make unsuccessful?
What we deliver

A structure your leaders agree on, with roles, ownership, and decision rights defined clearly enough to hold under pressure.

What's inside
  • Operating model design
  • Role and accountability mapping
  • Decision rights and governance
  • Workforce and capacity planning
  • Span of control review
  • Leadership team design
  • Change and communication planning
08·Reporting and Analytics

Numbers your board trusts and your operators can act on.

Most reporting problems are not tooling problems. They're definition problems. When two teams calculate the same metric differently, every meeting starts by arguing about the number instead of the decision. The dashboard gets rebuilt and the argument continues.

Questions worth answering
  • Do we agree on what our core metrics mean, in writing?
  • How long does the monthly package take, and how much of that is manual?
  • Can an operator see the number that their decision affects, without asking someone?
  • What does the board ask for that we can't produce quickly?
What we deliver

A defined metric layer, reporting that runs without heroics, and a board package that holds up to questions.

What's inside
  • Metric definition and data dictionary
  • Executive and board reporting
  • Operational dashboards
  • Data pipeline and warehouse review
  • KPI design
  • Reporting automation
  • Diligence grade reporting readiness
