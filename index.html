AI Strategy

Your AI should be producing operating value. Is it?

Most companies can tell you what they spend on AI. Far fewer can tell you what it returned. The spend is real, the pilots are live, and somewhere between the license and the workflow the value quietly went missing. Before you approve the next round of investment, these are the questions worth answering honestly.

  • What are we actually spending across licenses, infrastructure, and the people maintaining it?
  • Who is using it, how often, and for what? What did we buy twice?
  • Which workflows changed, and which ones look the same as they did before?
  • If we had to defend the ROI to the board next quarter, could we?

We answer those questions with numbers, not opinions. Then we fix what's broken, retire what isn't earning its keep, and build the operating model around what works.

Fintech Infrastructure

The architecture you choose now decides what you can do in three years

Payments, ledgers, KYC, sponsor bank relationships, and data architecture are not vendor decisions. They are ceiling decisions. The wrong one is survivable at ten thousand customers and structural at a million, and by then rebuilding costs more than the business can absorb. Our team has built and run this infrastructure inside American Express, Square, Citibank, Apple, and Walmart.

  • Does our architecture survive 10x volume, or do we already know where it breaks?
  • Can we add a product, a market, or a partner without a rebuild?
  • Where does customer and payment data live, who can reach it, and can we prove that?
  • What in our stack is a vendor decision we made once and never revisited?

We help you make these calls with people who have already lived with the consequences of making them, and we stay through the build.

Compliance and Governance

Regulated doesn't have to mean slow

In healthcare and financial services, compliance is where good roadmaps go to stall. Usually it isn't the regulation that slows you down, it's discovering the requirement late, treating every control as a one-off, and rebuilding evidence by hand every audit cycle. Teams who have done it before design for it once and keep moving. SOC 2, HIPAA, PCI, Section 508, state licensing, model governance.

  • Is compliance built into how we ship, or is it a scramble before every audit?
  • Which deals or partnerships are we losing to a certification we don't have yet?
  • Who owns governance for the AI and the data we've put into production?
  • If a regulator or an enterprise buyer asked for evidence tomorrow, how long would it take?

We build the controls, the documentation, and the governance so that the answer is days, not quarters, and so the next audit costs a fraction of the last one.
